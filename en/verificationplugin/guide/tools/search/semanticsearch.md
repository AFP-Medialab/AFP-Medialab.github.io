## General information

**Fact Check Semantic Search** allows you to search existing fact-checks using natural language queries rather than exact keyword matching. It uses semantic similarity to find relevant fact-check articles even when the wording differs from your query.

The tool searches across a database of fact-checks published by verified fact-checking organisations to help you quickly determine whether a claim has already been investigated.

## How to use it

Enter a claim, question, or piece of text in the search field. The tool returns a ranked list of existing fact-checks that are semantically similar to your query, along with links to the original articles.

## How it works

The tool embeds your query using a language model and compares it against pre-computed embeddings of fact-check articles. Results are ranked by semantic similarity score.

## Use cases

- Quickly checking whether a viral claim has already been debunked
- Finding related fact-checks to a story you are investigating
- Avoiding duplication of existing fact-checking work

## Tips

- Try rephrasing your query if initial results are not relevant
- The tool works best with complete sentences or claims rather than single keywords
